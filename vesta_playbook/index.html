Acquisition Target Characteristics

✓ Strong signals
  • Proprietary vertical software (not generic SaaS, not reseller)
  • Mission-critical: regulatory, payroll, compliance, workflow-embedded
  • Recurring revenue: maintenance contracts, licensing, support
  • 10–30 years in market, surviving multiple economic cycles
  • 5–150 employees, founder-owned, clean cap table
  • Government or enterprise clients
  • Microsoft / Cisco / Oracle / AWS partner for 10+ years
✗ Disqualifiers
  • No proprietary IP — just services or consulting
  • VC-backed or prior PE involvement
  • Primary product is a tool they resell (VAR without own IP)
  • Under 10 employees (too person-dependent)
  • Growth-stage, still finding product-market fit
  • B2C focus
  • Founded after 2015

Decision Maker Psychographic

This is the founder or controlling shareholder. Not a hired CEO. The person who built it over 15–25 years.

🏗

The Founder-Operator

50s–60s · Technically fluent · Built first version themselves · Spanish-dominant

What they care about

  • Legacy over liquidity — wants the company to survive, not just to get paid
  • Employees they've worked with for 10–15 years
  • Client relationships they've built personally (especially gov/enterprise)
  • Staying relevant post-transaction — not being sidelined

Trigger moments

  • Approaching 60 with no succession plan
  • Key technical person just left — realized how fragile the knowledge base is
  • Larger competitor entered their vertical
  • A peer in their network just sold and it went well
  • Health or family event that changes priorities

Vesta's strongest cards

  • Buy-and-hold, never sold a company — exact opposite of PE
  • Founder stays, runs the business post-acquisition
  • 12 acquisitions already in LatAm — social proof in their region
  • No pressure to grow for exit — patience to invest in the business

What shuts them down immediately

  • Generic "we acquire companies in your space" cold email
  • English-first contact when Spanish-dominant
  • Leading with multiples or valuation early
  • Anyone who feels like a flip buyer
  • Outreach that shows you didn't look at the product

Acquisition Signals

These are the observable events that indicate a founder is approaching a transition moment — even if they haven't said it. Monitor these per target, not just at first research.

High-priority signals

Act fast
  • Founder hires a GM or COO for the first time. They're creating distance from day-to-day. Transition thinking has started.
  • Key technical co-founder or CTO leaves. The institutional knowledge is gone. Fragility is now visible to the founder personally.
  • Competitor from outside the region enters their vertical. A well-funded entrant threatening their core market accelerates urgency.
  • Founder posts about "legacy," "next chapter," or milestone anniversaries. Transition is on their mind. Receptive window is open.
Watch and warm
  • Long-tenure employee promoted to Director or VP. Testing internal succession — the external option is also being considered.
  • First-ever sales or BD job posting. Trying to grow but hitting a ceiling. They need capital or a network they don't have.
  • Founder LinkedIn dormant for years, then reactivates. Posting about the business or their journey — often correlates with transition thinking.
  • Speaking at a local tech association event (CAMTIC, ANTTI, AGEXPORT). Seeking visibility. Open to conversations they wouldn't take cold.

How to monitor without manual effort

LinkedIn
Follow the founder. Turn on post notifications for Tier 1 targets. Check company page quarterly for new job postings and employee title changes.
Google Alerts
Set alerts for the company name and founder name. Catches press mentions, contract awards, speaking engagements — in Spanish and English.
Procurement DBs
Check quarterly. A company winning new government contracts is healthy. Silence for 12+ months is worth flagging — especially for targets with heavy public sector exposure.
Timing: The goal is to be in conversation before the trigger hits, not after. A founder who just lost their CTO is already under pressure — a cold outreach in that moment lands badly. A founder who gets a warm, specific first contact 6 months earlier will remember you when the moment comes.

How to Find Companies Like 3Tech

Standard databases miss a large share of these targets. The best ones have thin English-language digital footprints and don't show up in Apollo or RocketReach searches. Use four sources in parallel, then enrich with RocketReach.

Layer 1 — Partner directories

Any software company in Central America that has been a Microsoft, Cisco, Oracle, or AWS partner for 10+ years is worth knowing. ISV status means they've built on the stack — not just reselling it.

Layer 2 — Government procurement databases

3Tech won their eCarCheck contract through the ATTT — that's a public procurement record. A company winning recurring government software contracts is almost always bootstrapped, mission-critical, and founder-owned. Search for software vendors, not products.

Panama: panamacompra.gob.pa → search "software" → filter by vendor El Salvador: comprasal.sv Costa Rica: sicop.go.cr Guatemala: guatecompras.gt Honduras: honducompras.gob.hn

Layer 3 — Local tech association directories

Companies that have been members for 10+ years are established and founder-owned. Newer members are growth-stage startups. Also worth contacting association staff directly — they know who the established players are in each vertical.

Most of the best targets don't appear in English-language databases. These searches surface companies with 20-year-old websites, local SEO, and zero international exposure — targets Vesta's competition hasn't found yet.

Outreach That Works in This Market

Central America is a small market. The founder of 3Tech Panama probably knows 10 people who know you. Volume-based cold outreach doesn't work here — a bad first impression spreads fast.

What works
  • Warm introductions. A referral from a shared contact — local law firm, Microsoft account manager, chamber of commerce officer — is worth 10 cold emails. The market is small enough that everyone knows everyone.
  • Partner channel intros. Microsoft, Cisco, AWS, Oracle local account managers know all their ISV partners personally. The highest-quality first contact you can get.
  • Recognize the work first. Reference a specific product, a client vertical they serve, or an award they won. Show you actually looked. Don't open with "we acquire software companies."
  • Spanish-first contact. Even if they respond in English, writing first in Spanish signals respect. For a founder who's spent 25 years building in Panama, that matters.
  • LinkedIn InMail (targeted). Less saturated than North American markets. A thoughtful InMail that shows you read their profile has a noticeably higher response rate here.
  • No acquisition talk in message 1. Open with curiosity. "How do you see the next 5 years for STEPS?" — not "we'd love to explore an acquisition." Let them bring up succession when ready.
What doesn't work
  • Generic templates: "we're interested in acquiring companies in your space" — they've seen it.
  • English-only outreach when the founder's market and team are Spanish-speaking.
  • Leading with valuation or multiples before trust is established.
  • Volume-based cold email campaigns. A bad rep spreads fast in a small market.
  • Any signal that you plan to restructure, rebrand, or replace leadership.

First message framework

Line 1 — Specific observation. About their product, a client vertical they serve, or something they built. Not flattery — an informed observation that shows you actually looked.

Line 2 — One sentence on Vesta. "Adquirimos empresas de software vertical en LatAm de forma permanente — nunca hemos vendido una empresa."

The ask. Not "are you interested in selling." Instead: "¿Tendría sentido una conversación de 20 minutos? Sin agenda más allá de entender mejor el negocio."

Length: under 100 words. Founders in this market don't read long emails from strangers.

RocketReach Workflow

A specific sourcing workflow for finding 3Tech-type targets. The problem with standard B2B databases in Central America: the best targets have low English-language digital footprint. This workflow layers RocketReach with sources it can't see.

Where RocketReach Works vs. Where It Fails Here

RocketReach works well
  • Finding founder/CEO contact info once you know the company
  • Bulk enrichment when you upload a company list from elsewhere
  • Chrome extension: enrich LinkedIn profiles in real-time
  • Job title filtering: Fundador, Director General, CEO
  • Saved search alerts when new matching profiles appear
RocketReach blind spots
  • Misses companies with thin English web presence
  • Industry tagging is unreliable for CA IT services firms
  • Government software vendors often categorized as "consulting"
  • Can't surface companies from govt procurement databases
  • Missing founders who aren't on LinkedIn
Best approach: Use external sources (procurement DBs, partner directories, Spanish-language search) to build the target list first — then use RocketReach to enrich it with contact information. Don't rely on RocketReach's own search as the primary discovery layer.
