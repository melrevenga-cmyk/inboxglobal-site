01 — The situation

You have the product.
What's missing is the system.

AIS has a proven service, strong market positioning, and a 3,000-name PE contact list. The gap is not credibility — it's a reliable, repeatable mechanism to surface the right buyers at the right moment, before a competitor gets the call.

What's working — protect it
Referral and WOM pipeline is proof the service delivers. Website messaging is strong. The 3,000-name list is a real, underutilized asset. HubSpot infrastructure is in place. The incoming content strategy (PMI checklist, M&A guides) is exactly right for this audience.

Nine months of SEO with limited pipeline return. No system in place to detect and act on pre-LOI deal signals in near real-time. Template-based HubSpot outreach reads as generic to a PE audience. Apollo Professional recommended as a consolidated, cost-effective prospecting and signal platform with native HubSpot integration.
The core problem: PE buyers don't search for integration consultants. They call 2–3 people already on their mental short list. AIS needs to build that familiarity before the LOI lands — not after.

03 — Ideal customer profile

Three buyer tiers.
One priority target.

These tiers are built on market logic, not assumptions about a specific list — because we haven't seen the list yet. Step one of the engagement is segmenting the 3,000 contacts and identifying how many actually fall into each tier. The pilot is scoped conservatively around whatever Tier 1 subset we find.

Tier 1 · Primary target
Operating Partner / VP Portfolio Ops
PE firm · active add-on acquirer
Integration quality is their personal scorecard. When an add-on goes badly, they're the ones in the room explaining it to Managing Partners. They have budget authority and feel urgency — both are required for a fast, high-value sale. First priority once we see the list.
Tier 2 · Strong influence
VP of M&A / Integration Director
PE-backed platform company · post-LOI phase
Once an LOI is signed, this person executes the integration day-to-day. They have strong influence over vendor selection even when the PE firm holds budget approval. They're often more reachable and more willing to have a conversation than a PE partner — and they know firsthand what a failed integration looks like.
Tier 3 · Long-term nurture
Managing Director / Deal Partner
PE firm · thesis-driven acquirer
These are investors, not operators. They set strategy and approve large budgets, but they rarely manage vendor relationships directly. Critically — they are the hardest to reach cold. They work through networks. They belong in a long-term nurture strategy, not a pilot outreach sequence.
Note: Tier composition will be confirmed once the 3,000-name list is reviewed. Pilot volume is scoped conservatively around the Tier 1 subset we actually find — not assumed.

06 — Deliverability infrastructure

Before a single email sends,
the foundation has to be right.

Sending cold outreach to PE executives from an unwarmed domain is a fast way to get blacklisted and permanently damage AIS's sender reputation. The infrastructure below is non-negotiable — and it's built with this specific audience's trust instincts in mind.

Two dedicated .com sister domains
Two dedicated .com sister domains registered and forwarded to aisintegration.com — e.g. aisadvisory.com and aispartners.com. SPF, DKIM, DMARC, and BIMI records configured on each. 2–3 mailboxes per domain. Primary domain never touched, never at risk.
Mailbox warmup — 3 weeks minimum
All sending mailboxes warmed using Instantly over 21 days. Volume starts at 5 emails/day per mailbox and scales to 35–40/day. Warmup pool interactions build sender reputation organically before any live outreach begins.
Inbox placement testing before go-live
Mail-Tester and GlockApps verify inbox placement across Gmail, Outlook, and Exchange. Blacklist check across major RBLs. Spam score review — copy adjustments made if needed. No sequence goes live until placement is confirmed clean.
Ongoing monitoring throughout pilot
Weekly deliverability health checks for 12 weeks. Bounce rate hard ceiling: 2%. Unsubscribe handling automated. Domain reputation dashboard shared with AIS monthly. Any flag addressed within 24 hours.
07 — Sequence strategy

What PE executives actually
respond to.

PE principals delete "we help PE firms with post-merger integration" in under 3 seconds. Every sequence is written to sound like it came from a peer who noticed something specific — not a vendor with a pitch.
Sequence A — Pre-LOI signal triggered (4 touches · 21 days)
Day 1 Email 1 — Signal-specific, no preamble. Subject line references the specific trigger — the platform deal that just closed, the fund that just announced, the role that just posted. Body establishes AIS's relevance in one sentence tied to that specific context. Ends with a single, low-friction question. Under 75 words. No attachments.
Day 5 Email 2 — Useful, unrequested. Drops a relevant AIS resource — PMI Readiness Checklist or a sector-specific integration note — with no preamble and no meeting ask. Framed as: "This is specific to what you just announced, not a general pitch." Anyone who opens it has self-identified.
Day 12 Email 3 — Specific risk, specific ask. Names a concrete integration failure mode common to their sector — multi-site ERP migration, dual-payroll overlap, revenue leakage from misaligned sales teams. Asks for 20 minutes to compare notes, framed as peer-level knowledge sharing, not a vendor pitch. One link, no deck.
Day 21 Email 4 — Clean close. Acknowledges the timing may be off. Tells them AIS will be relevant when the next add-on closes — not as a pitch, as a fact. No warmth theater. Two sentences, done.
Sequence B — Proactive Tier 1 list outreach (4 touches · 28 days)
Day 1 Email 1 — Portfolio observation, not an introduction. Leads with something specific and verifiable about their add-on activity or sector concentration — a deal pattern, a geography, a holding period. Shows the homework without narrating it. One sentence on AIS's relevant experience. No ask, no deck link.
Day 7 Email 2 — A specific insight, not a content drop. Shares a short AIS Insights piece or integration framework relevant to their sector focus — framed as "made me think of your [sector] platform given what we've seen," not as a newsletter. The resource has to be genuinely useful to this person's actual situation. No meeting ask.
Day 16 Email 3 — A real situation, briefly described. References a specific integration scenario AIS navigated — no client name, but specific enough to be credible: the challenge, the timeline pressure, the outcome. Not a testimonial format. Ends with a single meeting ask grounded in that context, not a generic CTA.
Day 28 Email 4 — Acknowledges reality, keeps the door open. Notes the timing probably isn't right. States plainly that AIS is worth a conversation when the next deal is in motion. No performance of warmth. One or two sentences — and it actually reads that way.
